## History & Development

Development of the Drift Life project began in May 2021, when Hong Kong designer Kenny Yeung started sharing early experiments for a new rear-wheel-drive drift chassis. The first public posts showed little more than basic 3D-printed concept parts used to validate the suspension geometry and overall drivetrain layout before a complete prototype existed.

Just weeks later, the project had already evolved into a functional prototype built from approximately 90% FDM 3D printed parts. During this stage, different motor positions, wheelbase configurations and rear suspension concepts were evaluated while the chassis underwent continuous refinement through track testing. By July 2021, after roughly one month of development and several design revisions, the project had already reached its fourth prototype (V4).

Development continued throughout the following months, with the designer describing more than seven months of testing between the original prototype and the next generation of the chassis. Successive versions, including V1.5 and V2, introduced numerous refinements while gradually transitioning from a fully 3D printed prototype toward a production-oriented design incorporating carbon fiber chassis plates.

The final production version, released as the DL One in July 2022, retained many of the ideas developed during the prototype phase, including a highly adjustable suspension, wide wheelbase compatibility and a compact gear-driven drivetrain. An installation manual and setup guide were published alongside the release, while HRC Mini-Z Arena became one of the primary retailers for the chassis.

Community reception was generally positive regarding the chassis' engineering ideas and adjustment options, particularly the rear suspension geometry and extensive tuning features. However, several early users criticized the quality of the FDM-printed components, reporting inconsistent print tolerances, bearing fitment issues and relatively soft plastic parts. As a result, opinions often concluded that while the underlying design was innovative, the manufacturing quality prevented the chassis from reaching its full potential.
